# Velocity Fraud

## What it is
Velocity fraud is an unusually high frequency of transactions on the same account within a short window of time — multiple charges within minutes or hours, rather than the normal spread of purchases across days or weeks. A legitimate cardholder rarely transacts back-to-back at that pace, which is what makes a burst of activity a strong signal on its own, independent of any single transaction's amount.

## How it typically shows up
Once a card or account is compromised, a fraudster usually moves fast. Sometimes that means several small "test" purchases in quick succession to confirm the stolen credentials still work before attempting something larger. Other times it means simply making as many purchases as possible before the account is frozen or the real owner notices. Either way, the result is a tight cluster of transactions packed close together in time — often spread across different, unrelated merchants — instead of the account's usual spaced-out pattern.

## Evidence signals
- Two or more transactions on the same account within a short window (e.g. under 24 hours), when the account's normal history shows far less frequent activity
- A small "test" transaction followed shortly by a noticeably larger one
- Transactions landing across unrelated merchant categories in quick succession, rather than the kind of single-category shopping trip a real customer would make
- A burst that breaks from the account's established pace of activity, regardless of whether the individual amounts look normal
